Foundations

Rhythm Lab

Read and tap rhythms against a metronome, with timing feedback on every single tap.

What it trains

Rhythm Lab shows a rhythm, counts you in, and listens to your taps on the screen or the space bar. Each tap is marked early, late or on time, in milliseconds. A short calibration step measures your device’s audio and touch delay, so the feedback is accurate on a laptop, tablet or phone.

  • Reading rhythms with whole, half, quarter, eighth and sixteenth notes, dots, rests and triplets
  • Simple, compound and irregular time signatures: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 5/8, 6/8 and 7/8
  • Keeping a steady pulse, including through bars where the metronome goes quiet
  • Recognising written rhythms by ear

Exercises

Read and tap
A rhythm appears. After a count-in, tap it along with the metronome.
Steady pulse
Tap every beat. Some bars drop the metronome so you keep time on your own.
Rhythm by ear
Hear a rhythm and pick the matching notation from three options.

Try it

A short interactive demo built from the same code as the real tool.

Settings you can change

  • Time signature: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 5/4, 5/8, 6/8 or 7/8
  • Tempo from 40 to 180
  • Rhythm level 1 to 5, from quarter notes up to sixteenths, syncopation and triplets
  • One or two bars per exercise, and one or two count-in bars
  • Metronome subdivision on or off
  • Timing window: relaxed, standard or strict
  • Latency calibration, with a fine-adjust slider
  • Session length

Instruments and clefs

Rhythm notation on a single-line staff. Tap on the screen pad, click, or use the space bar. Sounds: metronome click and a soft tap tone.

Reference view

A rhythm reference lists every note and rest value with its counting, and plays any example at your tempo.

Who it’s for

  • Players of any instrument who want to read rhythms accurately
  • Anyone whose timing drifts when the metronome is not there
  • Students meeting 6/8, 5/4 and 7/8 for the first time

Who it isn’t for

  • Drummers looking for kit grooves or rudiments
  • Anyone wanting a standalone metronome app — the metronome here serves the exercises

What you need

  • A modern web browser on a computer, tablet or phone
  • Speakers or headphones
  • The ability to hear audio — this tool includes ear training that depends on listening

About the sound: all instrument sounds are synthesized in your browser. They are not recordings of real instruments.

In every tool

  • Instant feedback on every answer, with ✓ and ✗ marks and the correct answer shown.
  • Settings that save themselves, plus named practice sets you can switch between.
  • A session summary when you finish, with your accuracy and the items to review.
  • A reference view for looking things up without being quizzed.
  • Progress and streaks: accuracy over time and your weakest items, stored only on your device.
  • Progress backup: move your progress to another device with a backup code or file.
  • Works offline once unlocked, and installs to your home screen or desktop.
  • Keyboard shortcuts, visible focus, a larger-interface setting, and dark mode.
