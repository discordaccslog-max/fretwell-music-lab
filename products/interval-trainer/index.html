Foundations

Interval Ear Trainer

Hear the distance between two notes and name it — ascending, descending or together.

What it trains

Interval Ear Trainer plays two notes and asks you to name the interval between them. You choose which intervals are in play, whether they are played upward, downward or at the same time, and how the difficulty grows. A reference mode lets you compare any two intervals side by side whenever you want to.

  • Recognising every interval from the minor 2nd to the octave, including the tritone
  • Hearing intervals melodically (one note after the other) and harmonically (together)
  • Telling apart intervals that are easy to confuse, such as major and minor 3rds or perfect 4ths and 5ths

Exercises

Ascending intervals
The lower note plays first, then the upper note.
Descending intervals
The upper note plays first, then the lower note.
Harmonic intervals
Both notes play together.
Mixed
Any of the three, chosen at random each time.
Compare (reference mode)
Pick two intervals and play them from the same starting note, one after the other, as often as you like.

Try it

A short interactive demo built from the same code as the real tool.

Settings you can change

  • Custom interval sets: tick exactly the intervals you want, and save sets to reuse
  • Adaptive difficulty: starts with a few clearly different intervals and adds more as your accuracy rises, or eases back if it drops
  • Direction: ascending, descending, harmonic or mixed
  • Starting note: random, or always from C
  • Register: low, middle or high
  • Tone: keyboard or plucked guitar
  • After a wrong answer: hear your answer and the right answer back to back
  • Session length

Instruments and clefs

Keyboard and plucked-guitar tones. After each answer the interval is shown on the treble staff and the piano keyboard.

Reference view

The compare view plays any two intervals from the same note, with both shown on the staff and keyboard.

Who it’s for

  • Players who want to recognise intervals by ear, whether they play guitar, piano or something else
  • Students who find some intervals easy and others hard, and want practice weighted towards the hard ones

Who it isn’t for

  • Anyone who cannot hear audio — this tool is entirely ear training
  • Players looking for compound intervals and chord-based ear training (those are in the Atelier Edition)

What you need

  • A modern web browser on a computer, tablet or phone
  • Speakers or headphones
  • The ability to hear audio — this tool includes ear training that depends on listening

About the sound: all instrument sounds are synthesized in your browser. They are not recordings of real instruments.

In every tool

  • Instant feedback on every answer, with ✓ and ✗ marks and the correct answer shown.
  • Settings that save themselves, plus named practice sets you can switch between.
  • A session summary when you finish, with your accuracy and the items to review.
  • A reference view for looking things up without being quizzed.
  • Progress and streaks: accuracy over time and your weakest items, stored only on your device.
  • Progress backup: move your progress to another device with a backup code or file.
  • Works offline once unlocked, and installs to your home screen or desktop.
  • Keyboard shortcuts, visible focus, a larger-interface setting, and dark mode.
